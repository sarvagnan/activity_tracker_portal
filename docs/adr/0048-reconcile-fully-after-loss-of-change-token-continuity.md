# Reconcile fully after loss of change-token continuity

An expired or invalid per-type changes token means the Sender can no longer prove continuity with the source change stream. Before first publication, the incomplete attempt and its evidence remain retained but abandoned, none of it publishes, and a new token plus Initial Snapshot identity starts a full new attempt. Abandoned Attempt Evidence remains inspectable under an explicit condition and filter, but cannot become current or contribute unless independently reacquired through a completed attempt. After an established baseline, the Sender obtains and durably commits a fresh changes token before starting the first page of a full Reconciliation Snapshot. The full page chain produces a reconciliation-tagged Snapshot Completion, then a catch-up Change Cycle drains that pre-read token; both completion messages are required before the reconciled set is eligible for activation. Previous summaries remain visible with a reconciliation-pending condition while new evidence is matched by Source Identity, and retained records are neither deleted nor replaced merely because continuity was lost. A previously current identity absent from this completed pair becomes a Reconciliation Absence only when the same Sender Installation retained unchanged full-history permission, record-type scope, and origin scope throughout; it may withdraw the prior contribution without pretending Health Connect emitted a Source Retraction. Any uncertainty about coverage blocks activation instead. When reconciliation cutover work begins, it atomically freezes a Cutover Target at the newest then-completed evidence boundary and creates an append-only Candidate Build under named processor versions; later completed cycles remain pending instead of repeatedly restarting reconciliation. After every targeted record has a terminal processing outcome, one transaction activates the reconciled source selections, qualified absences, canonical selections, and affected Health Summaries and appends a Cutover Record. Integrity, uncertain coverage, and incomplete work create a retained Reconciliation Failure and leave the prior view current with a non-dismissible reconciliation-failed condition until a governed reconciliation succeeds or the affected retained data is explicitly deleted. Bounded transient execution retries remain automatic, but retry after Reconciliation Failure requires explicit Operator intent through the same reconciliation Interface, exposed by the Operations page and `portal` command; it creates a successor Candidate Build against the same Cutover Target under corrected governed behavior and never edits failed output. When that build requires a corrected processor version, its successful Compound Cutover also activates a successor Processor Generation reusing all unchanged mappings, and the Cutover Record carries both reasons and selectors; unrelated upgrades remain queued. While reconciliation remains unresolved, later Change Cycles may archive and process but cannot publish onto the pre-reconciliation view. After successful cutover, one ordinary publication catches up to the newest then-completed evidence boundary before the next queued target begins, without waiting for an open cycle. If the existing target cannot be corrected, an explicit Start Fresh Reconciliation action retains and supersedes it, obtains a new pre-read token, and requires another full snapshot and catch-up; pending cycles and other queued changes remain unpublished behind the fresh target. Lost source continuity likewise requires this fresh path, and no generic cancellation or force activation exists; disabling collection, retiring the Sender, or acknowledging the condition does not resolve it. Terminal quarantine, Policy-Pending, unresolved, or failed outcomes do not block activation, and safe contributions publish with explicit conditions and counts. This costs a complete reread, duplicate evidence, explicit absence semantics, staged state, and reconciliation cutovers in exchange for closing the reread race and avoiding an arbitrary recent-time heuristic that could miss old updates or retractions.
